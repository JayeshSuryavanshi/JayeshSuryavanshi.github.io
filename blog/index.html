    The wrong opponent

    Jev ships with two headline multipliers, 193.6x faster and 444.6x cheaper, and neither one names what it is faster and cheaper than. Arithmetic on TypeSafe’s own published cells pins them to two different opponents, one of which is more accurate than Jev. Sixteen configurations on that chart and not a single small model. I did the sums, then ran the missing baseline on gold labels on my own laptop, and found that the most-cited evidence against small encoders is largely a truncation bug.

    The empty classroom

    A deep learning professor at IISc put down his prepared lecture and spent the hour asking his students why they still come to class. He is also the one automating the answer away: no research assistants, his own learning moved to an LLM, and a skill on his company’s marketplace built to teach in his voice. The alarm is worth more coming from a builder, not less.
  • Research Notes · Jul 2026

    The unmapped majority

    Most tools for geotagged social data quietly map the 1-2% of posts with exact coordinates and call it “the geography.” Count the coverage first: on a realistic 5,000-post city pull, 84% carry no location, place-resolution recovers 10× more than exact coordinates alone, and the honest move is to report the hole. It is the idea I built GeoSocialX around.
  • Research Notes · Jul 2026

    Informative but subsumed

    Does news text help forecast social unrest? Measured properly (336M events, 55 countries, leakage-controlled out-of-time backtests, a signal ladder up to neural embeddings of 1.37M headlines), the answer is +0.3-0.6 AUPRC points, sourced from the arithmetic of coverage, not its semantics. The meaning of the news is already in the event record.
